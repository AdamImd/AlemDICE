\begin{abstract}
Large language-model agent teams need to allocate work without exposing private
state to a central planner or allowing communication cost to grow unchecked.
We present a bounded decentralized team-formation protocol for Alem, a
long-horizon multi-agent survival and crafting environment.  Agents exchange
canonical task announcements, bids, awards, and acceptances through a public
control ledger; a deterministic allocator uses only claimed public information,
and ordinary communication is restricted to locked task teams.  A six-agent
hosted-model screen completed all 12 scenario cells and formed 15 of 15
true-feasible teams, with exact whole-allocation agreement in 10 cells and two
successfully repaired invalid decisions.  We then transfer the protocol to
Alem while preserving the source agents' observations and physical action
space.  In matched 200-step episodes at 8, 16, and 32 agents, the protocol
increased absolute team return by 23.0\%, 67.4\%, and 38.8\%, respectively, and
increased coordinated first task completions at every scale.  The gain exhibits
diminishing marginal yield beyond 16 agents.  Because the high-scale comparison
currently contains one common seed per condition, these embodied results are
descriptive mechanism signals rather than estimates of statistical
significance.  The artifact records failed attempts, communication traffic,
provider usage, resolved configuration, and deterministic replay evidence to
support a larger paired study.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Language agents can interpret unfamiliar objectives and adapt their behavior
without task-specific retraining, but this flexibility does not itself solve
multi-agent allocation.  Full-team broadcast grows quadratically in delivered
traffic, independent agents duplicate easy work, and unconstrained natural
language makes team membership difficult to audit.  A central planner can
reduce ambiguity, but it introduces privileged state, a bottleneck, and a
single point of failure.

We study whether agents can instead form bounded task teams through a
decentralized public protocol.  The protocol separates two channels: a small
canonical control plane for recruitment and a team-scoped natural-language
channel for execution.  Its design is informed by Contract Net
\citep{smith1980contractnet}, but accepted transitions are replicated and
deterministic rather than entrusted to an unverified coordinator.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a strict, replayable team-formation protocol with bounded records,
    task leases, and communication isolation;
    \item a public-information joint allocator and a true-information oracle
    used only for evaluation; and
    \item an evaluation sequence from provider-free mechanical gates to a
    hosted six-agent screen and matched Alem scaling at 8, 16, and 32 agents.
\end{itemize}

\section{Environment and Source Baseline}

We build on Alem, a procedurally generated, long-horizon multi-agent survival
and crafting environment with ordinary and explicitly coordinated
achievements \citep{tessera2026alem}.  Physical agents receive local
role-specific text observations, select from currently legal actions, retain a
bounded private scratchpad, and communicate through delayed natural-language
messages.  Soft specialization is enabled and shared reward is disabled.

The source condition uses the original collaborative prompt, observation
renderer, action parser, action space, and one model call per active physical
agent per tick.  Our treatment does not add a planner action, hidden map access,
or privileged capability truth.  It changes only team formation and message
routing.  This boundary is important: an apparent coordination gain is not
meaningful if the treatment also receives better world state or physical
controls.

\section{Decentralized Team Formation}

\subsection{Canonical public records}

Each control message is one UTF-8 line of at most 256 bytes using the TFP1
schema.  Record kinds include task announcement, call for proposals,
application or bid, award, acceptance, lock, cancellation, completion, and
expiry.  Every kind has an exact field set and canonical field order.  The
parser rejects unknown, duplicate, reordered, oversized, or non-canonical
fields rather than repairing them silently.

A task card contains a public task identifier, three-dimensional demand,
required team size, reward, deadline, and sponsor.  The experiments in this
paper cap required teams at three agents.  Sender identity and round are
trusted transport-envelope metadata and cannot be overwritten by generated
text.

\subsection{Replicated lifecycle}

Every agent applies valid public records to the same deterministic directory.
The directory validates phase, sender authority, membership, exclusivity, and
deadline constraints.  A roster becomes active only after the required
acceptances produce a lock.  Locked membership is task-bound and expires or is
cancelled through explicit transitions.  Canonical serialization and an audit
hash allow independent replicas to detect divergence.

Before a lock, agents may transmit only bounded control records.  After a lock,
ordinary execution messages are delivered only among members of that task
team.  This makes the communication boundary enforceable rather than a prompt
request.

\subsection{Allocation without private truth}

The treatment computes a joint allocation from public task cards and submitted
claims.  It enumerates bounded, non-overlapping candidate rosters and orders
solutions lexicographically by completed public reward, claimed roster
utility, raw claimed cost, and stable identifiers.  Therefore agents that share
the same public ledger compute the same allocation without a central model
call.

For evaluation only, a separate oracle repeats the optimization with true
capabilities and costs.  Oracle information never appears in agent prompts,
control records, or treatment selection.  We report reward regret, utility
regret, raw-cost delta, and exact-roster agreement against this oracle.

\section{Evaluation}

\subsection{Staged protocol}

E0 verifies variable population shapes, legal-action round trips, role cycling,
communication accounting, saved-state replay, and artifact completeness with
scripted agents and zero provider calls.  E1 measures the unchanged source
baseline at \(N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\), three common seeds, and 200 requested steps.
E2 evaluates recruitment in controlled six-agent scenarios.  E3 transfers the
selected mechanism to Alem and compares source and treatment at
\(N\in\{8,16,32\}\), common seed 14100, and 200 steps.

The high-scale cells presently contain one episode each.  We consequently
report paired descriptive effect sizes and no significance test.  The low- and
high-scale campaigns use different seeds and are not treated as one causal
curve.

\subsection{Outcomes}

The primary embodied outcome is absolute team return
\[
R_{\mathrm{team}} = N\bar R_{\mathrm{agent}}.
\]
We also count first task completions summed across agents, team-unique task
types, and the decomposition into individual and coordinated completions.
Normalized percentages diagnose efficiency but do not replace absolute output.
Reliability measures include valid episodes, attempts, parse failures,
transport errors, model calls, token usage, latency, and communication bytes.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Hosted recruitment screen}

The hosted six-agent screen completed 12 of 12 cells across four scenario
families and three seeds.  All 15 locked teams were truly feasible.  Ten cells
matched the exact whole allocation and 12 of 15 task rosters matched the
oracle.  Mean true-utility regret was 0.00566.  The run made 293 logical calls
with no transport or incomplete-response failures.  Two of 291 initial
decisions were invalid; both were repaired to valid abstentions.  These results
establish protocol usability and bounded allocation quality, not embodied
efficacy.

32 agents but does not eliminate per-agent efficiency decay.

\section{Limitations and Next Experiments}

The Alem treatment comparison has one seed per scale, so the observed gains may
reflect seed-specific geometry or stochastic model behavior.  Task-completion
counts are first unlocks rather than repeated throughput.  The protocol relies
on claimed information and can therefore allocate poorly when claims are
strategic or incorrect.  We have not yet established long-horizon role
coherence, robustness to network partitions, or behavior at 1,000-step
horizons.

The confirmatory study will repeat every high-scale cell over common seeds and
report paired intervals.  At 32 agents it will also compare one global team
with multiple independent teams while holding physical population and task
requirements fixed.  Additional ablations will isolate bid-closure timing,
team-scoped communication, and formation overhead.  These tests are necessary
before claiming robust scalability or persistent role alignment.

\section{Reproducibility}

The anonymous artifact contains named configurations, exact seeds, a dependency
lock, curated machine-readable results, deterministic protocol tests, and
scripts that regenerate the figures and tables.  Paid launchers fail closed on
a dirty working tree and record resolved configuration, source revision,
attempt ledgers, provider usage, and failures.  Installation and manuscript
builds never issue model requests.
