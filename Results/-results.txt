Leader: 


• The trace suggests the leader is adding coordination friction rather than useful global control. This is
  more specific than “extra calls cost more.”

  Concrete evidence:

  - Noop increased from 203 to 291 actions: 33.8% → 48.5%.
  - Productive Do actions fell from 68 to 48: −29%.
  - Request Drink rose from 5 to 29, suggesting more time spent stalled or managing survival.
  - Communication-route content grew from roughly 110k characters to 522k—about 4.8×.
  - Both normal and coordination achievements declined, so the leader did not merely trade exploration for
    better teamwork.

  - Baseline reached progression milestones that leader_peer missed, including collecting diamond, defeating
    an orc mage, firing the bow, and the coordinated large-passive kill.

  The central problem appears to be conflicting control signals. At step 10, for example, the new boss plan
  instructed agents to assemble around a three-agent tree, while peer messages in the same worker prompt
  still described scouting east, holding beside the table, and transferring wood. Those messages were
  reasonable when written, but stale after the boss replanned. Workers therefore receive both a fresh
  authoritative assignment and obsolete peer intentions.

  There are several likely contributors:

  - Boss plans are too tactical. Exact movement sequences become stale quickly in a partially observed,
    simultaneous-action environment.

  - The five-step cadence creates open-loop control: the boss cannot correct a blocked movement or changed
    resource state until the next refresh.

  - Peer and leader channels duplicate information and can disagree.
  - Every worker message is routed both to peers and the boss, while full observations are also sent to the
    boss. The resulting context is large and repetitive.

  - Agents may wait for an ideal synchronized configuration instead of pursuing safe local progress,
    explaining the elevated Noop count.

  - The leader has no direct action, so every planning call must improve worker decisions enough to justify
    its overhead. In this run it did not.

  Most promising improvements, in order:

  1. Make the leader strategic, not tactical
     Assign objectives such as “miner obtains a wood pickaxe” or “agents 0 and 2 coordinate on eastern
     stone,” but let workers choose movement actions locally.

  2. Establish one source of authority
     When a new boss plan arrives, expire older peer intentions. Workers should treat peer messages as
     status reports, not competing plans.

  3. Use event-driven replanning
     Refresh when a milestone succeeds, an assignment becomes infeasible, a worker reports a blocker, or the
     world changes materially. Keep a longer maximum timeout as a fallback.

  4. Add worker override and feedback
     Workers should be allowed to reject an assignment with a structured reason such as blocked,
     target_missing, resource_changed, or unsafe. The boss can then replan immediately.

  5. Compress communication
     Send the boss structured deltas—position, inventory, target, blocker, health, and last result—instead
     of full rendered observations. Forward peer messages selectively rather than duplicating all of them.

  6. Add plan commitment and completion conditions
     Preserve the current objective unless there is evidence it failed. Each assignment should have explicit
     success and abort conditions, preventing unnecessary plan churn.

  7. Use synchronization protocols only when needed
     For coordinated actions, designate a rendezvous, readiness signal, and execution turn. Outside those
     moments, allow independent progress.

  The leader_no_peer result will clarify the diagnosis:

  - If it beats leader_peer, stale or redundant peer communication is a major cause.
  - If it remains below baseline, the leader’s tactical planning, cadence, or information representation is
    the main issue.

  - If achievement improves but cost remains high, the architecture works behaviorally but needs sparse/
    event-driven boss calls.

  Given this trace, I would test a fourth variant next: a strategic, event-driven leader with structured
  worker reports and no tactical movement instructions.
